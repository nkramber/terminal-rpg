# The setting

Status: world file, v1. Owner: Nate. Started 2026-09-12 in the world-building interview (OQ-18). Written in ASD-STE100 (D-10).

This file holds the world of the game: its history, its land, its powers, and its rules. Its source is the decision register, entries D-123 onward. Each fact cites its decision. When a decision changes, this file changes in the same PR.

The text that the player reads has its own voice (D-11). This file gives facts for writers, not player text. A quoted line in this file is a sample, not a string.

## Feel and limits

- Final Fantasy Tactics sets the feel: church and crown politics, class war, betrayal, and a story that turns on faction choices (D-125).
- The world copies no plot device of FFT (D-136, D-140). The file `banned-devices.md` lists the devices.
- Text shows violence and cruelty in short physical detail. It never shows sexual violence, and it never shows harm to a child on screen (D-126).
- Every plotline converges at the end of the game. No region ends with the destruction of its own faction (D-131).

## The war and the treaty

A war between two crowns ran for twenty years and ended six years ago (D-154). The old crown signed region one over to the enemy crown in a treaty (D-127). The treaty also brought the church of the enemy crown, and that church banned the old faith (D-137).

The peace cost the region. Unpaid soldiers turned bandit, the old lords bent the knee to the new crown, and commoners pay both sides (D-127). The bandits still roam after six years, because the passes pay: tolls, ore trains, and travelers make banditry a trade (D-249).

## The land and its people

Region one is a land of mountain passes (D-153). It is high and cold, with mining towns along the passes. The miners dug the old ground deeper here than anywhere else, and the war dug deeper still (D-128).

Humans are the only people in the world. No elves, dwarves, or beast-folk exist. Crown, faith, and class make every difference between people (D-157).

## The thing below

Under the old ground lies a thing that answers spilled blood. The war dug into that ground and woke it (D-128). A few powerful people know it and use it. It stays rare in region one, and it is never a stock demon (D-128, D-155).

All magic draws on the thing below, and a caster does not need to know it (D-148). A licensed mender and a hanged hexer tap the same source. Every caster feeds the thing a little. Whether a battle rule tracks that feeding is open (OQ-35).

Holy and dark are names that people give to two ways the thing below answers (D-158). Each church claims the holy side. Battle text keeps the two words, and the story treats them as claims. No text presents holy damage as proof of a god.

## The two churches

The foreign church came with the treaty. Its leaders know that their god is a lie, and they use the faith for power (D-137). The old faith of region one believes that its god is real. The player can never be sure of either, and no world file confirms or denies either god.

The loss of identity under the ban is part of the plot (D-137). The young grew up under the ban, and the old remember the faith (D-154).

The foreign church holds the license law (D-130, D-149). It trains and licenses menders. A caster with no license is a hexer, and hexers hang. A healer of the old faith has no foreign license, so the law calls that healer a hexer.

## The waystones

A waystone is an upright stone from an older age. Near a waystone, the thing below goes quiet (D-134). In game text, a save point is a waystone. Documents keep the term save point.

The old faith tended the waystones, and its rites kept the thing below quiet (D-141). Nobody can say whether the rites or the stones did the work. The foreign church calls the stones idols and breaks them. It does not know what the stones hold back.

The stones stay fixed in the ground, and they never carry the evil (D-140). Every waystone that serves as a save point stays whole in the story (D-141).

The age of the stones has no firm answer (D-156). The old faith says its founders raised them. The foreign church says they are pagan relics of the age that built the machines. Nobody can prove either claim, and no world file settles it.

## The older age and its machines

An older age built machines, and a few of them survive (D-129). The airship is one: a salvaged wonder, not an industry. Nobody knows the date of the older age (D-156).

Outside those machines, the technology is late medieval (D-129). People fight with crossbows and plate. The large cities have a printing press.

## Lessons and the law

No character has a job (D-268). A character gains abilities from lessons: rites for spells and drills for physical skills (D-275, D-278). A lesson teaches a form and holds no power. The file `cast.md` gives the rules of lessons and aptitudes.

The license law reaches the rites a character uses (D-130, D-275). A rite with a church stamp is legal, and a rite with no stamp can hang its user. The guild marks its wardens, and a warden can lose the mark (D-294).

How a party gets a stamped rite is open (OQ-42). Whether a rule in play tracks an unstamped rite is open too (OQ-45).

## Rules that stay out of the story

Some rules exist in battle and on menus alone, and the story never explains them (game-text-style rule 12).

- A down is a battle rule. No story line mentions it, and a death in a story scene is real and separate (D-135).
- A save point is a waystone in game text (D-134).

## Enemies of region one

Most enemies are people: bandits, soldiers, church wardens, and hired blades (D-155). Beasts hunt the roads. Near spilled blood, a few things appear that have no place in the world. People fight with real tactics (D-35), and the text carries the weight of every killing (D-126).

## Names

Names come in two sound palettes (D-159). A person takes one or two syllables, a foreign person can take three, and a place takes up to three (game-text-style rule 11, D-300).

The samples show the sound. They are not cast names.
